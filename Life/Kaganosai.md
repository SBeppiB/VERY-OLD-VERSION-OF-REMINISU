Kaganosai is led by a Kyariaūman who’s age, name, literally anything personal about her is undefined. Her role is to keep the seal stable and for the kaganosai society to stay hidden. She wears a kimono mask, and her dark leather dress is adorned with fur hood, and teeth. 

  

Her role is very straightforward; guarding the seal's immense power. Preserving its stability overall and to also enforce the secrecy and survival of Kaganosai’s enclosed culture

  

Although her attire seems quite intimidating, under her fur hood, are beautiful flower patterns and many gems attached to ribbons under her belt, which has the daggers of her most powerful enemies

Careers

  

Valkyries are women who were appointed guards and warriors from the Kyariaūman, tasked with patrolling Kaganosai's islands, and to defend their people.

  

Shrine Maidens are young girls who are appointed as religious priests by their Bishops. Due to the nature of Kaganosai’s women, some of their power comes from the belief collected within the seal. By constantly praying and casting rituals for the seal, they gain more power.

  

Geisha's can range from as young as 21 to many years of age. These lovely ladies can scavenge power from unfortunate men by seducing or tricking them into their traps, often luring them with false desires and delusions. They are playful people, despite their aggressiveness. They'd only target men out of personal reasons or for desperation. The Geisha’s are well known for their use of dark magic.

  

Shirabyoshi - Kind of like Geishas, doing dancing and illusion tricks with their graceful movements. However, they’re more oriented around Elemental Magic, meaning they’re used for Arc reinforcements against threats and terrorists

  

Oiron - The highest echelon of Geishas, they are not only known for beauty and grace, but their ability to extract power from high-status men. Through their charm and tact, they can lure well-respected and powerful men into false security, only to siphon their strength, memories, even their souls. They can use aura manipulation to reflect the desires and dreams of their victims

The Kaganosai culture is kind of strict, as their society is entirely female. This is because of their source of power, the seal, a crystal, an object, something that possesses great power, creates them. Children born of man, fathered by male outsiders, or conceived outside the seal's influence are considered impure. 

  

These unfortunate souls are deemed tainted by the corruption of the morals of man, and by the age of eight, they are exiled from Kaganosai, left as castaways with a naginata and a kimono for survival. This harsh judgement shows and reflects the societies fear of impurity, and high standards for secrecy

Every girl from Kaganosai undergoes a mandatory coming-of-age ritual, by the age of 13, she will spend a 3 moons meditating near the seal, and 5 moons venturing to a treacherous region called tynde salv, a small country full of many dangers to the people of kaganosai, especially the blood-lustful beasts. Her main goal? Collect the materials needed to make her own shield, to fend for herself in the land where many perish. 

  

Within her shield, the girl must have an arc crystal (popular choices usually involve Bloody ruby or Aqualabrite) in the center of her shield, to release the arc energy needed to defend herself.

  

After fighting off the potential dangers, she will come back to Kaganosai, and show her shield to the Valkyries. If her shield is fully intact, she will be free, and be able to stay in her home. If her shield is partially chipped at the edges, she will be required to go back and fix it, usually by the edge of the island. If it is severely damaged, or if the arc crystal core is broken or lost, she will be forced back into the island, and make a new one.

Kaganosai children, despite their harsh upbringing, often find humor in crafting crude clay sculptures of people they resent—usually their former oppressors or rivals.

  

 Using whatever materials they can scavenge, they shape exaggerated features like oversized noses or comically evil grins, ensuring their targets are as ridiculous as possible. 

  

Once complete, these clay effigies become the victims of a gleeful barrage of magic. The girls laugh wildly as fireballs, wind slashes, or bolts of light pulverize the sculptures into dust, the air thick with the smell of scorched clay. 

  

It's a cathartic, chaotic spectacle, blending childlike playfulness with raw magical power, offering a fleeting sense of vengeance and relief.
